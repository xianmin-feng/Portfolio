Nibi — AI Wellness Companion

Designing a wellness app that bridges AI, health data, and emotional design into one iOS experience

iOS SwiftUI AI-Native Design Multi-LLM Integration Personal Project

Why I Built This

After tracking my own wellness across multiple apps, I noticed a pattern: I had plenty of health data but never acted on it. Dashboards showed numbers I'd glance at and forget. AI chatbots gave generic advice disconnected from my actual state. As a designer working at the intersection of AI and product, I saw an opportunity to explore a different model -- one where the app already understands how you're doing and communicates through context-aware, ambient interactions instead of charts.

The Design Challenge

Design a wellness experience built around calm intelligence: filter multiple health signals into a single clear output, connect the dots across metrics (poor sleep + elevated stress = a breathing nudge), and deliver insight through agentic, anticipatory interactions -- not dashboards users have to interpret themselves.

The Solution

Nibi is an AI-native iOS wellness companion that transforms raw health data into personalized, context-aware interactions. Rather than presenting charts, Nibi assembles a real-time picture of your day -- sleep, stress, hydration, movement, heart rate -- and uses it to power every experience: an AI companion that already knows how you're doing before you say a word, ambient nudges that surface at the right moment, and a generative UI layer where the avatar, colors, and score all adapt to your current state.

The name "Nibi" comes from the Japanese word for "you" -- because the app is entirely about you. Your habits, your mood, your pace.

Design Decisions

  • Zero dependencies -- Pure SwiftUI, SwiftData, HealthKit. No third-party bloat. Every icon is hand-drawn as a custom SwiftUI Shape for visual consistency.
  • Privacy first -- All data stays on-device. No accounts, no sign-ups, no data collection. AI chat only activates with your own API key.
  • Multi-provider AI -- Supports Claude, Gemini, OpenAI, Llama, Groq, and Together AI. Users choose their preferred model. Without a key, Nibi still works with thoughtful built-in responses.
  • Emotional design -- The floating Nibi avatar blinks, changes expressions, and can be dragged anywhere on screen. Small details that make the experience feel alive.
  • Scoring integrity -- Every core metric always counts toward the wellness score. Logging just one thing won't inflate your number -- the system uses a fixed denominator to stay honest.

Design Process

Calm Intelligence: Data In, Clarity Out

Nibi tracks 13 metrics daily but never shows a wall of numbers. A single wellness score (0-100) distills everything into one glanceable signal. Mood-mapped colors cascade across the UI so users see their state without reading labels. The design philosophy: filter health data into actionable insight while preventing cognitive overload.

Agentic Interactions Beyond the Chat Box

The AI doesn't wait for users to ask questions. Ambient nudges anticipate needs -- hydration reminders when intake is low, wind-down prompts when stress is high. The avatar's expression shifts based on wellness data, creating a passive feedback loop. These aren't notifications; they're ambient interactions that surface at the right moment without demanding attention.

Probabilistic UX: Honest Health Communication

Health data is inherently uncertain. The wellness score uses fixed denominators so logging one metric can't inflate the number -- the system stays honest about what it doesn't know. Each metric contributes a weighted portion, and the score label ("Excellent," "Fair," "Getting started") communicates confidence without false precision.

Generative UI Driven by Health Data

The interface isn't static -- it's generated from the user's state. The avatar's expression, the color palette, the AI's tone, and the dashboard layout all adapt in real time. Ask "How am I doing?" and the AI assembles mood, sleep, stress, hydration, streak, and weekly trends into a personalized response. Every surface is a reflection of the user, not a template.

Designing for Presence: Guided Breathing

The 4-7-8 breathing exercise is an interaction design problem: guiding physical behavior through a screen. The visuals become the breath -- a soft orb expands on inhale, holds, and contracts on exhale. The screen shifts to a dark, minimal palette to signal a mode change. Progress stays subtle so users stay present, not monitoring.

How I Built It

I used Cursor + Claude as my primary development workflow -- designing the experience, then describing interactions in plain language and iterating in real time. The process followed a tight loop: describe → implement → test → refine. I'd test on the simulator, identify friction points, and articulate fixes conversationally -- getting precise, targeted results within minutes.

This AI-assisted approach let me focus on design judgment and UX decisions while rapidly validating ideas through functional prototypes. The result: a polished, production-quality app built end to end -- concept, interaction design, architecture, and implementation.

The Takeaway

Nibi reinforced that the hardest part of AI-native design isn't the technology -- it's shaping how intelligence feels. Every decision, from the adaptive avatar to the honest scoring system, came back to one question: does this make the person feel seen, not surveilled?

This project demonstrates what I bring to a team: the ability to identify a design opportunity, architect an end-to-end AI experience, build functional prototypes to validate it, and ship a polished product -- bridging design thinking, health data, and AI into a cohesive, human-centered experience.
