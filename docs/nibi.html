Nibi — AI Wellness Companion

Designing a wellness app that feels like a conversation, not a spreadsheet

iOS SwiftUI Multi-LLM AI Personal Project

The Problem

Every week starts with good intentions -- drink more water, sleep better, manage stress, take breaks. By midweek, most of it is forgotten. Existing tools felt either too cold (habit trackers that feel like spreadsheets) or too disconnected (chatbots that know nothing about your day). Nothing bridged the gap between tracking and understanding.

The Insight

Wellness isn't a dashboard problem -- it's a relationship problem. People don't need more charts and red warning icons. They need something that notices when they haven't had water in hours, knows they slept poorly, and responds with warmth instead of judgment. The tool should feel less like a spreadsheet and more like a conversation.

The Solution

Nibi is a lightweight iOS companion that connects daily wellness tracking with context-aware AI conversations. Ask "How am I doing today?" and instead of a generic response, Nibi knows you only slept 5 hours, your stress is high, and you've only had 2 glasses of water. Every response is grounded in your actual data.

The name "Nibi" comes from the Japanese word for "you" -- because the app is entirely about you. Your habits, your mood, your pace.

Design Decisions

  • Zero dependencies -- Pure SwiftUI, SwiftData, HealthKit. No third-party bloat. Every icon is hand-drawn as a custom SwiftUI Shape for visual consistency.
  • Privacy first -- All data stays on-device. No accounts, no sign-ups, no data collection. AI chat only activates with your own API key.
  • Multi-provider AI -- Supports Claude, Gemini, OpenAI, Llama, Groq, and Together AI. Users choose their preferred model. Without a key, Nibi still works with thoughtful built-in responses.
  • Emotional design -- The floating Nibi avatar blinks, changes expressions, and can be dragged anywhere on screen. Small details that make the experience feel alive.
  • Scoring integrity -- Every core metric always counts toward the wellness score. Logging just one thing won't inflate your number -- the system uses a fixed denominator to stay honest.

Design Process

Color as Emotional Language

Most wellness apps use color for decoration. In Nibi, color is the interface. Each mood state maps to a distinct color that cascades across the entire experience -- from the check-in buttons to the avatar to the dashboard. When a user selects "rough," the UI shifts to warm red tones without needing a single word of explanation. This means users can glance at their screen and immediately understand their emotional state reflected back at them. The same principle applies to individual metrics: hydration, movement, sleep, and stress each have their own color identity, so the dashboard becomes scannable at a glance -- reducing cognitive load and letting users focus on how they feel, not on reading charts.

Reducing Friction in Daily Check-Ins

The biggest UX risk in a wellness app is abandonment. If the daily check-in feels like a chore, users stop coming back. I designed the flow around one principle: one question per screen. Instead of presenting a dense form, each of the 9 metrics gets its own focused moment -- large tap targets, no scrolling, no decision fatigue. A progress indicator at the top gives users a sense of momentum without creating pressure. Adaptive encouragement ("You're doing great!" / "Almost there") responds to where they are in the flow, reinforcing completion. For missed days, a catch-up feature offers preset templates so users can backfill without guilt -- because a broken streak shouldn't feel like failure.

Designing an Emotional Companion

Static mascots feel dead. I wanted Nibi's avatar to feel like something you could develop a relationship with. The key insight was layered imperfection: the avatar breathes, floats, and glances around on independent rhythms, so its movement never loops predictably. It blinks at random intervals. Most critically, its expression responds to your wellness data -- a wide smile when you're thriving, a gentle concerned look when you're struggling. This creates an ambient feedback loop: users don't need to open a stats page to know how they're doing. The avatar shows them. It turns a data-driven app into something that feels like it cares.

Conversational AI That Knows You

Most AI chat experiences start from zero context every time. Nibi's core interaction design challenge was making the AI feel like it already knows you. Before every conversation, the app assembles a complete picture -- today's mood, how you slept, your stress level, hydration, movement patterns, your streak, and your week-long trends. When a user asks "How am I doing?", the response references their actual day. The AI's personality was designed to feel like a supportive friend: warm and slightly playful, never clinical or preachy. It celebrates small wins ("You hit your water goal three days in a row") and gently acknowledges hard days without judgment. The goal was to make users feel seen, not assessed.

Guided Breathing as Interaction Design

Breathing exercises are inherently an interaction design problem: how do you guide someone's physical behavior through a screen? I used the 4-7-8 technique and designed the visuals to become the breath. A soft orb expands as you inhale, holds still, then contracts as you exhale. The entire screen -- color, opacity, scale -- moves in sync with the breathing rhythm, creating a visceral connection between the interface and the body. The environment shifts to a dark, minimal palette to reduce stimulation and signal a mode change: this is no longer a productivity tool, it's a calming space. Cycle indicators show progress without interrupting the flow, so users stay present instead of checking how much time is left.

How I Built It

The entire app was built in a single day using Cursor as my IDE paired with Claude as the AI coding assistant. The workflow was conversational -- describe what I wanted, iterate in real time, refine through back-and-forth. Features that would normally take weeks came together in hours.

The describe → implement → test → refine loop ran in minutes. The UX polish phase was especially effective -- I'd test on the simulator, notice something that felt off, describe it in plain English, and get a precise, targeted fix. That tight feedback loop is what made it possible to go from functional to polished quickly.

The Takeaway

Building Nibi sharpened my conviction that great wellness tools need emotional intelligence, not just data. The hardest design challenge wasn't technical — it was making an AI feel warm and trustworthy enough that someone would share how they're really feeling. Every decision, from the living avatar to the honest scoring system, came back to one question: does this make the person feel seen?

On the craft side, Nibi proved I can take a product from zero to polished — concept, design, architecture, and implementation — by pairing deep design intuition with AI-assisted development. Knowing what to build matters as much as knowing how to build it.
