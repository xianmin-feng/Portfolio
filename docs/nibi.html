Nibi — AI Wellness Companion

Designing a wellness app that feels like a conversation, not a spreadsheet

iOS SwiftUI Multi-LLM AI Personal Project

The Problem

Every week starts with good intentions -- drink more water, sleep better, manage stress, take breaks. By midweek, most of it is forgotten. Existing tools felt either too cold (habit trackers that feel like spreadsheets) or too disconnected (chatbots that know nothing about your day). Nothing bridged the gap between tracking and understanding.

The Insight

Wellness isn't a dashboard problem -- it's a relationship problem. People don't need more charts and red warning icons. They need something that notices when they haven't had water in hours, knows they slept poorly, and responds with warmth instead of judgment. The tool should feel less like a spreadsheet and more like a conversation.

The Solution

Nibi is a lightweight iOS companion that connects daily wellness tracking with context-aware AI conversations. Ask "How am I doing today?" and instead of a generic response, Nibi knows you only slept 5 hours, your stress is high, and you've only had 2 glasses of water. Every response is grounded in your actual data.

The name "Nibi" comes from the Japanese word for "you" -- because the app is entirely about you. Your habits, your mood, your pace.

Design Decisions

  • Zero dependencies -- Pure SwiftUI, SwiftData, HealthKit. No third-party bloat. Every icon is hand-drawn as a custom SwiftUI Shape for visual consistency.
  • Privacy first -- All data stays on-device. No accounts, no sign-ups, no data collection. AI chat only activates with your own API key.
  • Multi-provider AI -- Supports Claude, Gemini, OpenAI, Llama, Groq, and Together AI. Users choose their preferred model. Without a key, Nibi still works with thoughtful built-in responses.
  • Emotional design -- The floating Nibi avatar blinks, changes expressions, and can be dragged anywhere on screen. Small details that make the experience feel alive.
  • Scoring integrity -- Every core metric always counts toward the wellness score. Logging just one thing won't inflate your number -- the system uses a fixed denominator to stay honest.

Design Process

Emotional Color System

Color isn't decorative in Nibi -- it's functional. Every mood maps to a specific color: teal for "great," mint for "good," yellow for "okay," coral for "low," and red for "rough." These colors cascade through the entire UI -- the avatar, the wellness score, the check-in buttons -- so the app always reflects how you feel at a glance. Stress levels shift along a green-to-red gradient as you drag the slider. Even the metrics have dedicated colors (sky blue for hydration, orange for movement, purple for sleep) so you can scan your dashboard without reading a single label.

Check-In Flow: Progressive Disclosure

The daily check-in walks through 9 steps -- mood, sleep, hydration, meals, movement, stress, bonus activities, heart rate, and notes -- but never feels like 9 steps. Each screen shows only one question with large, tappable inputs. A row of capsule indicators at the top fills in as you go, giving a sense of progress without pressure. Contextual helper text adapts to your input ("You're doing great!" or "Almost there"), and the whole flow is designed to complete in under two minutes. There's also a catch-up feature with preset templates for missed days, so a skipped check-in doesn't break your streak or your motivation.

The Living Avatar

The Nibi avatar isn't a static icon -- it breathes, blinks, and looks around. A breathing animation gently scales the body every two seconds. Floating motion bobs it vertically on a separate rhythm. The eyes blink at random intervals (every 2.5 to 5 seconds) and glance in different directions, creating an uncanny sense of presence. Most importantly, the avatar's expression responds to your wellness score: the mouth curves from a wide smile to a concerned frown, and the eyes shift height to match. These layered animations run on staggered timings so nothing feels mechanical -- the avatar feels alive.

AI Personality Design

The AI chat isn't a generic chatbot dropped into a wellness app. I designed a detailed system prompt that defines Nibi's personality as "warm, caring, empathetic, slightly playful" -- like a supportive friend, not a therapist. Before every conversation, the app injects your full wellness context: today's mood, sleep hours, stress level, hydration, movement, heart rate range, streak count, and even your 7-day mood average. So when you ask "How am I doing?", the response is grounded in real data. The prompt also sets boundaries: no emojis, no clinical language, responses kept to 2-4 short paragraphs. The goal was an AI that notices patterns and celebrates small wins without ever feeling robotic or preachy.

Breathing Exercise: Synced Visuals

The guided breathing screen uses the 4-7-8 method (inhale 4 seconds, hold 7, exhale 8). The visual design mirrors the breath: a concentric three-circle orb expands to full size on inhale, holds steady, then shrinks on exhale. The gradient colors shift with each phase, and opacity dims during the hold and exhale to reinforce the calming rhythm. A large countdown timer and phase label ("Inhale," "Hold," "Exhale") keep you oriented, while cycle indicator dots show your progress. The dark background (#1A1630 with a radial gradient) was chosen to reduce visual stimulation and create a meditative environment.

How I Built It

The entire app was built in a single day using Cursor as my IDE paired with Claude as the AI coding assistant. The workflow was conversational -- describe what I wanted, iterate in real time, refine through back-and-forth. Features that would normally take weeks came together in hours.

The describe → implement → test → refine loop ran in minutes. The UX polish phase was especially effective -- I'd test on the simulator, notice something that felt off, describe it in plain English, and get a precise, targeted fix. That tight feedback loop is what made it possible to go from functional to polished quickly.

The Takeaway

Building Nibi sharpened my conviction that great wellness tools need emotional intelligence, not just data. The hardest design challenge wasn't technical — it was making an AI feel warm and trustworthy enough that someone would share how they're really feeling. Every decision, from the living avatar to the honest scoring system, came back to one question: does this make the person feel seen?

On the craft side, Nibi proved I can take a product from zero to polished — concept, design, architecture, and implementation — by pairing deep design intuition with AI-assisted development. Knowing what to build matters as much as knowing how to build it.
